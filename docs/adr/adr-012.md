# ADR-012 — Direct publishing and primary Admin Studio access management

Status: Accepted (explicit user direction, 24 September 2026).

Supersedes the mandatory image attribution/publication validation in the 23 September review and the mandatory Publisher MFA rule. Does not change the Supabase/PostgreSQL architecture.

- Writers, Editors and Publishers can access Studio at AAL1 with a valid active session and verified email, including when they have a previously enrolled factor. Admins retain MFA enforcement except existing approved UUID-bound exceptions. An account with both Admin and an editorial role still follows Admin policy. Reader accounts with enrolled MFA retain their existing policy.
- The primary Admin recorded in `private.access_policy` can grant/revoke Writer, Editor or Publisher roles through `/studio/access`. Existing account email is a lookup input; target and actor UUIDs are the authorization/audit identifiers. No Admin grants, MFA-exception creation, email-confirmation override, Auth-table mutations or outbound invitations are exposed by this operation.
- Role changes are additive/removable per role and recorded in `private.access_audit`. Admin accounts are excluded from this management operation. Revoking one role does not remove another role displayed for that account.
- Image alt text, captions, credits, sources, rights, article summary, byline and public correction/update notes are optional. Existing values remain stored/rendered. No editorial approval is needed to publish. Optional review remains available.
- Save and publish is a single database transaction: create/save an immutable revision, then publish it. A failure rolls back the entire operation. Blank slugs are generated from the headline with a unique suffix. Existing canonical URLs remain stable.
- Authentication, ownership, current-version checks, supported content types, safe URL validation, request/upload bounds and nonempty headline/body remain technical safeguards. They prevent unauthorized changes, lost edits, broken records and resource exhaustion.

Consequence: a compromised editorial password/session can publish within that user's role without a second factor. Attribution/accessibility omissions no longer block publication; staff are responsible for supplying those details. Historical records, public notes already supplied, and author ownership remain protected.

Verification: isolated PostgreSQL role tests cover AAL1 Publisher operations, cross-writer denial, primary-Admin-only grants, grant revocation, audit entries, optional image metadata, generated slugs and atomic rollback. No test articles or grants are inserted into the hosted project.
